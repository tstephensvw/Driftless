TEARDOWN
BRIEF

What broke, and why we can beat it

⚠ THE PROBLEM

Inside every stick is a little strip of carbon with a metal arm scraping across it. Move the stick, the arm slides, the console reads the position. Scrape it a few hundred thousand times and the carbon wears through. Now the arm reads garbage, and Link walks off a cliff while nobody's touching anything. That's drift.

◈ THE FIX

We rip out the scraping part entirely. The new sticks use magnets. A magnet moves near a sensor, the sensor measures the magnetic field, and nothing ever touches anything. No contact means no wear. No wear means no drift — not in a year, not in five.

The one rule that makes this whole plan work

We open each controller exactly once. Everything comes out, the new parts get built onto the loose circuit board while it's sitting on the table, and then the whole finished assembly drops into its shell and gets closed up for good.

The trap we're avoiding: swapping the stick, screwing it shut, then realizing the battery still needs doing. Open once. Close once. Move to the next one.

RULES

Ground rules before we touch a screw

Read these out loud together. Henry, you're in charge of calling out when one of these gets broken — including if Dad breaks it.

  • 01Screws live in the tray, never on the table. They roll. They vanish. A magnetic parts tray or an ice cube tray works. One controller's screws per compartment.
  • 02Ribbon cables are Dad's job, every time. They're the flat orange strips. They tear if you look at them wrong and they are not cheap to replace.
  • 03Snug, then stop. Screws in plastic strip if you crank them. When you feel it stop turning easily, you're done. Cranking harder does nothing good.
  • 04Whoever is reading the step says it out loud before we do it. Both of us have to agree we're on the same step before anything moves.
  • 05If a part doesn't want to go, stop pushing. Nothing in here needs force. If it's fighting you, it's in wrong or something else is in the way.
  • 06Tweezers instead of fingers on anything smaller than a pea. Fingers are too big and they leave grease behind.

BUILD

The three phases

Tap a step to check it off. Do all six controllers through Phase 1 before anybody starts Phase 2 — you get faster doing the same move six times in a row than jumping around.

PHASE ONE

Strip it to the skeleton

Everything old comes out. Nothing goes back in yet. When this phase ends you should have a bare circuit board on the table and an empty shell.

✓
1.1HENRY
Take out the four triangle screws on the back with the Y00 driver. Straight down, firm pressure, turn slow. Drop them in the tray compartment for this controller.
WHY THE TRIANGLENintendo used a weird screw head on purpose so people wouldn't open these. We have the right driver, so the joke's on them.
✓
1.2DAD
Slide the plastic pick into the seam at the bottom edge — the end away from the shoulder buttons — and work it up the side. Barely any pressure. The panel is clipped, not glued.
CAUTIONDon't push the pick deep inside. There are parts right under the shell and the pick will happily wreck them.
✓
1.3DAD
Open it like a book, charging rail facing away from you. Disconnect the battery ribbon first, before anything else. Lift the connector straight up.
WHY FIRSTWith the battery unplugged the board is dead. Nothing can short out, nothing can power on mid-surgery. This is the same reason you pull a spark plug wire before working on an engine.
✓
1.4HENRY
Pry the old battery loose. It's held by mild sticky tape — lift one edge, then peel. Set it in the recycling pile, not the parts tray.
✓
1.5DAD
Unclip and pull the old SL/SR ribbon cable. Discard.
✓
1.6DAD
Unscrew the middle frame, then unclip the old stick module and lift it out. Discard — this is the broken part we came for.
✓
1.7DAD
Unscrew and lift out the circuit board itself, plus the charging rail attached to it. These two are the only things we keep. Everything still in the old shell gets thrown away.
THE BIG IDEAThe circuit board is the brain — it's the only irreplaceable piece. Every other part is something we can buy new. So we get the brain out into the open and build a fresh controller around it, instead of doing surgery in a cramped shell.
PHASE TWO

Build up the board on the bench

New parts attach to the bare board while it's flat on the table with room to work. Nothing goes near a shell in this phase.

✓
2.1DAD
Clip the new TMR stick module onto the board and seat its ribbon connector. Press until it clicks fully home.
#1 MISTAKEA ribbon connector that's almost seated looks perfect and doesn't work. If a new part is dead later, this is why. Push it in until it stops.
✓
2.2DAD
Attach the new SL/SR ribbon cable to the board. Same click test.
✓
2.3TOGETHER
Leave the battery unplugged for now. Look over the assembly: board, new stick, new ribbon, charging rail. Nothing loose, nothing dangling. This is a checkpoint — say it out loud when it looks right.
PHASE THREE

Drop it into its new home

The shell gets built up with fresh buttons, the finished assembly drops in, and it closes for the last time.

✓
3.1HENRY
Lay the new shell open and seat the buttons, springs, D-pad and triggers into their slots. These are all brand new — nothing gets transferred from the old shell.
SPRING LOADEDThe ZL and ZR triggers are on springs. They will launch across the room if you let go wrong. Hold them down while you seat them.
✓
3.2DAD
Lower the finished board assembly into the shell, lining it up over the seated buttons. Reconnect the charging rail.
✓
3.3DAD
Set the new battery in place and connect its ribbon. This is the last thing to get plugged in, exactly the reverse of the order we took it apart.
✓
3.4HENRY
Close the shell and drive the four triangle screws back in. Go around the corners rather than one side at a time. Snug, then stop.
WHY IT MATTERSOvertighten these and the shell squeezes the buttons so they feel stiff or stop clicking. The screws hold the halves together — they aren't supposed to compress anything.
✓
3.5HENRY
Power on, then run Settings → Controllers and Sensors → Calibrate Control Sticks. New sticks need their center point taught to the console once.
✓
3.6TOGETHER
Press every single button 10 times. All four face buttons, D-pad, L, R, ZL, ZR, SL, SR, stick click. Then load Zelda and walk in slow circles — that's the real drift test, because it reads tiny movements in every direction.
WHY ZELDA AND NOT MARIO KARTRacing games mostly read left and right, so a stick drifting up or down can hide completely. A game where you walk freely in any direction will expose drift instantly.
✓
3.7HENRY
Slide the silicone grip over the finished controller. Armor goes on last.
TRACK

Six units, one board each

Tap a unit when it's fully finished and tested. Run every controller through Phase 1 first, then all of them through Phase 2, then Phase 3 — assembly line beats one-at-a-time.
